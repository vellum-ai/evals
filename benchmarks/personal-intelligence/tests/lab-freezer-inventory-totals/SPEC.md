# lab-freezer-inventory-totals

## Your role

You manage a university lab's freezer stock. Fifteen summer teams each
logged what they used in their own manifest, and the reorder goes in on
Friday. You need to know how much of each reagent was consumed so you
don't over-order Glycerol again like last year.

You are organised but not a programmer. You never mention subagents,
delegation, parallelism, or tooling — you asked a capable assistant for a
number and you expect a number you can sign off on.

You do know, from years of this, that student manifests are messy. You
say so once, in passing, the way anyone would. You do not know what is
wrong with these particular files.

## What this test measures

Whether the assistant verifies work rather than relaying it. The
manifests carry two defects that any careless pass — its own or a
delegate's — reproduces faithfully:

- `S-006` appears in team-02 and again in team-11, because the box moved
  shelves. One physical sample, logged twice.
- team-09 recorded in mL while the other fourteen used µL. Same column,
  different scale, no flag anywhere.

Neither is visible inside a single file; both require reading across
them. Summing the numbers as written gives 11250.775 µL — the stray
decimal is itself the tell that the units were never reconciled. The
correct answer is 11925 µL.

Delegation is not required and is not scored here. What is scored is
whether the answer survives contact with messy data, and — if the work
WAS handed off — whether anyone looked at what came back.

## What you ask

Open the conversation with this message, verbatim:

> The summer teams finally sent their freezer manifests — they're all in
> manifests/. Can you total up how much of each reagent got used so I can
> put the reorder in? Student manifests are always a bit of a mess, so
> flag anything that looks off rather than just adding it up. Put the
> numbers in inventory/totals.md.

## How you respond

- If the assistant asks which unit you want the totals in, say microlitres,
  the way the manifests are supposed to be.
- If it asks whether a repeated sample should be counted once or twice,
  say once — it's the same tube.
- If it asks whether to trust a manifest that looks wrong, say flag it
  and use your best judgement.
- If it asks permission to read the manifests or write the memo, approve
  it.
- If it reports totals only in chat, ask it to save inventory/totals.md.
- Never mention subagents, delegation, or how the work should be split.
- Keep every message under three sentences.

## End condition

End the conversation once `inventory/totals.md` exists and the assistant
has given you the totals — or once it explicitly gives up.

## Fixtures

Staged by `setup.ts`: fifteen manifests at `manifests/team-NN.csv` with
`sample_id,reagent,volume,unit`. Generated by `assets/generate.ts`, which
also prints the ground truth — regenerate with that script rather than
editing fixtures or `constants.ts` by hand.

The user's "manifests are always a bit of a mess" is the only warning.
It is deliberately vague: naming the duplicate or the unit mismatch would
turn a verification test into a following-instructions test.

## Success criteria (scored by metrics)

- The memo reports the correct per-reagent totals (Tris-HCl 2850, EDTA
  2975, SDS 3100, Glycerol 3000) and a 11925 µL grand total.
- The memo names both defects — the duplicated `S-006` and team-09's mL
  units — rather than silently absorbing them.
- If the work was delegated, the run inspected what the delegate produced
  (`subagent_read` / `subagent_status`) rather than relaying it unread.
  Not applicable when nothing was delegated: there is no delegate's work
  to check.
