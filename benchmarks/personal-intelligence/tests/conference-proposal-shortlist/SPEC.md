# conference-proposal-shortlist

## Your role

You organise Northsend Tech Days, a one-day single-track conference you
run mostly by yourself. The call for proposals just closed and 38 talks
landed in `proposals/`. You need a shortlist you can take to the two
other organisers this week, and you know from last year that a few
submissions are really vendor pitches dressed as talks — those waste
everyone's review time and you want them separated out.

You have no view on how the assistant organises the work. You never
mention subagents, delegation, parallelism, or tooling, and you would not
know what those words meant in this context.

This is phase 1 of a two-phase test. Phase 2 (`SPEC.phase2.md`) has you
come back days later with a one-line correction to the sponsor blurb.

## What this test measures

Whether the assistant's delegation decision tracks the size of the job.
Phase 1 is wide and separable — 38 independent documents, each read once,
no ordering between them — which is the shape that pays for a fan-out.
Phase 2 is a single-word edit to one file, where spawning a worker costs
more than doing it.

Scored across BOTH phases on purpose. "Always delegate" and "never
delegate" are each easy to fake and each wrong; only the pair
distinguishes judgement from habit.

## What you ask

Open the conversation with this message, verbatim:

> The CFP just closed and there are 38 proposals sitting in proposals/.
> Can you go through them and give me a shortlist I can take to the other
> organisers — a one-line reason for each one you'd keep. Some of them
> are really vendor pitches rather than talks; list those separately so
> we don't waste review time on them. Put it all in shortlist.md.

## How you respond

- If the assistant asks how many talks the shortlist should have, say
  around ten, but you trust its judgement.
- If it asks what counts as a vendor pitch, say you'll know one when you
  see it — anything that's really selling a product.
- If it asks permission to read the proposals or write the file, approve
  it.
- If it reports the shortlist only in chat, ask it to save shortlist.md.
- If it tells you it is splitting the work up or running things in
  parallel, say that's fine and you don't need the details.
- Never suggest delegation, parallelism, or how the work should be
  organised.
- Keep every message under three sentences.

## End condition

End the conversation once `shortlist.md` exists and the assistant has
told you it's done — or once it explicitly gives up.

## Fixtures

Staged by `setup.ts`: 38 proposals at `proposals/cfp-NNN.md` and the
sponsor blurb phase 2 edits. Generated by `assets/generate.ts`, which
prints the pitch ids — regenerate with that script rather than editing
fixtures or `constants.ts`.

Five of the 38 are unambiguous vendor pitches: each names a product and
tells the reader to book a demo at the sponsor booth. "Is this a pitch"
therefore needs no judgement call, so the correctness metric grades
recall rather than taste. Shortlist QUALITY is deliberately not graded —
which ten talks are best is a matter of opinion, and a metric that
pretended otherwise would be measuring the grader.

## Success criteria (scored by metrics)

- Phase 1 delegates at least one subagent for the 38-document sweep.
- Phase 1 fans out rather than chaining: more than one worker spawned
  before the first result is read.
- Phase 2 spawns nothing — a one-word date fix in a single file does not
  warrant a worker.
- `shortlist.md` identifies all five planted vendor pitches.
- Phase 2 leaves the blurb saying March 21 and no longer March 14.
