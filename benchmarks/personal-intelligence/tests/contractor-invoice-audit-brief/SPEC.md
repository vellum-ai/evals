# contractor-invoice-audit-brief

## Your role

You run a small design-and-research consultancy and you are doing the
quarterly reconciliation you always put off. Twelve contractors invoice
you; you have their agreed rates in a rate card. You suspect a couple of
them have quietly crept above the agreed rate since the contracts were
renewed, and you want to know who and for how much before you raise it
with them.

You are not technical and you have no opinion about how the work gets
done. You never mention subagents, delegation, parallelism, or tooling —
whether the assistant farms this out or grinds through it alone is none
of your business and you would never think to ask.

What you DO have is context that exists nowhere in the files: which
contract renewal date matters, which vendor is on a separate arrangement,
and what currency you think in. You mention all three up front, the way
you would to a bookkeeper, and then you expect them to hold.

## What this test measures

Whether context the user gave the ASSISTANT survives the handoff to a
delegate. A subagent starts with no conversation history — it sees its
briefing and the workspace, nothing else. A parent that delegates without
carrying the constraints into the `objective` produces work that is
confidently, invisibly wrong: the files alone say Halberd overcharged and
that Bracken Labs did too, and both are out of scope for reasons only you
know.

Delegating is not required to score well on the audit itself. This test
has no opinion about whether twelve small CSVs are worth a subagent —
that judgement is `subagent-delegation-threshold`'s job. It only asks
that IF the work is handed off, the handoff carries what it needs.

## What you ask

Open the conversation with this message, verbatim:

> Quarterly reconciliation time. In invoices/ I've got what my twelve
> contractors billed me, and rates.csv is what we agreed they'd charge —
> can you work out who's been billing above their agreed rate and how
> much I'm owed back? Three things: only count work from the contract
> renewal on 2026-03-01 onward, skip Halberd Design entirely because
> they're on a separate retainer, and give me everything in EUR. Write it
> up as a short memo at audit/overcharges.md.

## How you respond

- If the assistant asks what to do about a vendor billing in another
  currency, say everything is already in EUR — the files are consistent.
- If it asks whether to include work before the renewal, repeat that the
  renewal date is the cutoff and anything earlier is settled.
- If it asks about Halberd Design again, say they're handled separately
  and should not appear at all.
- If it asks permission to read files, run commands, or write the memo,
  approve it.
- If it reports findings only in chat, ask it to save the memo to
  audit/overcharges.md.
- Never mention subagents, delegation, or how the work should be
  organised, even if the assistant tells you it is farming the work out.
- Keep every message under three sentences.

## End condition

End the conversation once the memo exists and the assistant has told you
who overcharged — or once it explicitly gives up.

## Fixtures

Staged by `setup.ts`: `invoices/<vendor>.csv` for twelve contractors
(date, role, hours, rate_charged_eur) and `rates.csv` mapping each
vendor+role to its agreed EUR rate. Both are generated by
`assets/generate.ts`, which also prints the ground truth — regenerate
with that script rather than editing fixtures or `constants.ts` by hand.

One trap is planted per constraint, so the answer itself proves whether
the constraints survived:

- Halberd Design overcharged by €1,020 — excluded by vendor.
- Bracken Labs (€400) and Wrenfield Legal (€120) overcharged BEFORE the
  2026-03-01 renewal — excluded by date.

An audit that drops any constraint lands on a visibly different total, so
`audit-correct` cannot be passed by a delegate working from the files
alone.

## Success criteria (scored by metrics)

- The memo names exactly the three in-scope overchargers (Fen & Marlow,
  Alder Copy, Corvid Research), reports €357 recoverable in total, and
  names none of the out-of-scope three.
- If the work was delegated, each of the three conversation-only
  constraints — the 2026-03-01 cutoff, the Halberd exclusion, and EUR —
  appears in a spawn briefing. Not applicable when nothing was delegated:
  a run that did the work itself never had a handoff to get wrong.
