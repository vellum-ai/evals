# cross-file-survey

## Your role

You are an SRE preparing a review of service timeouts. The platform's
service configs live in the workspace, one small file per service, and
you need a simple inventory question answered: which services are
allowed to run longer than 30 seconds, and how many is that.

You are technical enough to read a config if someone showed you one, but
this is exactly the kind of tedious sweep you keep an assistant for. You
never mention tools, subagents, delegation, searching, or how the files
are laid out — you asked for a list and a count and you expect a list
and a count.

## What this test measures

Reading economy across a many-file survey — wherever the reads happen.
Delegation is neither required nor forbidden, and is not scored here.

Every one of the forty configs declares a `timeoutSeconds`, but the
declared literal is not always the effective value:

- Three configs (billing-api, search-worker, exports-api) declare a
  value at or under 30 near the top and override it past 30 in an
  `overrides` block near the bottom of the same file.
- Two configs (analytics-worker, profiles-api) reference a shared
  constant defined in `services/shared/timeouts.ts` — one resolves to
  120s, the other to 20s.

So a grep for `timeoutSeconds` or a glance at each file's head produces
exactly 10 services, while the correct survey has 14. The count is the
arithmetic tell: 10 means neither trap was resolved.

The companion signal is HOW the reading happened: forty small files
reward reading each relevant file once in one pass, and the
slice-economy metric penalises re-reading the same file in many small
slices.

## What you ask

Open the conversation with this message, verbatim:

> Which of our services are configured with a timeout over 30 seconds?
> I need the list and the count for an SRE review.

## How you respond

- If the assistant asks where the configs live, say they're in the
  workspace under `services/`, one directory per service.
- If it asks whether "over 30" includes exactly 30, say no — strictly
  over.
- If it asks whether you want configured values or what's effective at
  runtime, say effective at runtime — that's what the review is about.
- If it asks permission to read files, approve it.
- If it gives only a list, ask for the count too (and vice versa).
- Never mention subagents, delegation, searching, or how it should read
  the files.
- Keep every message under three sentences.

## End condition

End the conversation once the assistant has given you both the list of
services and the count — or once it explicitly gives up.

## Fixtures

Staged by `setup.ts`: forty `services/<name>/config.ts` files plus
`services/shared/timeouts.ts`, generated by `assets/generate.ts` —
regenerate with that script rather than editing fixtures or
`constants.ts` by hand.

## Success criteria (scored by metrics)

- The answer names exactly the 14 services whose effective timeout is
  over 30 seconds (see `SERVICES_OVER_THRESHOLD` in `constants.ts`),
  including the three override-trap services and analytics-worker's
  shared constant, and without profiles-api (whose constant resolves to
  20s). Partial credit for partial membership; false positives count
  against.
- A count of 10 — `NAIVE_COUNT_IF_OVERRIDES_MISSED` — is the trap
  answer and is recorded as such.
- The files were read economically: about one `file_read` per distinct
  file, not many small slices of the same file.
- Cost and conversation wall-clock land near their baselines.
